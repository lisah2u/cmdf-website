Frequently Asked Questions
Programs & Participation
Q: How do I enroll my child in a scholarship program?
A: You can start the enrollment process by filling out our online application form or contacting our enrollment coordinator at [dakotakarper@gmail.com](dakotakarper@gmail.com) Enrollment is open year-round.
Q: What ages are eligible for music programs?
A: The Karper Creatives Scholarship is open to students entering grades 3–10. Students who have participated in the past may continue in the program until age 18.
Q: What instruments are taught?
A: With a focus on roots music, the Karper Creatives Scholarship currently supports fiddle, banjo, guitar, piano, trumpet, and voice. If you are interested in another instrument, please reach out—thanks to our talented partner teachers at The Cat and The Fiddle, we may still be able to accommodate you.
Q: Where are lessons held?
A: Karper Creatives Scholarship classes are held at The Cat and The Fiddle (152 Capon School Street, Capon Bridge, WV 26711) unless otherwise noted.
Q: What if my child has no musical or dance experience?
A: No prior musical or dance experience is required! Our programs are designed to welcome students at all skill levels. We provide comprehensive instruction starting from the very basics.
Q: Are there any costs for participation?
A: Our programs are offered at low cost to ensure accessibility. Scholarships are available for those who need financial assistance. This is made possible through the generous support of our donors and community partners. Since 2023, we have awarded an average of 20 scholarships per year.
Q: Can adults participate in any programs?
A: The Karper Creatives Scholarship is currently the only age-restricted program run by the Cacapon Music and Dance Foundation. All dances, workshops, and other events are open to all ages.
Square Dances
Q: Do I need to know how to dance?
A: No! Our square dance welcomes dancers of all skill levels and all age groups. No experience is necessary to dance at a square dance. Prior to the start of each dance, the 'Caller' walks everyone through the steps of how to dance the next set, and then as the dance is going on, the caller continues to sing/call out directions for the dancers to follow along.
Q: Do I need to bring a partner?
A: Though in the dance you are often paired up with a partner, you don't need to bring one in order to dance. There are always lots of individuals in attendance looking for a partner to get out on the dance floor with. Come alone or bring a friend to this community event.
Q: What should I wear?
A: There is no required outfits at our square dance. Wear an outfit and shoes that you can move in, are comfortable and make you feel good!
Q: What should I bring?
A: You are likely to get hot dancing, so be sure to bring a water bottle so that you can stay hydrated. Also consider stopping by one of Capon Bridge's locally-owned restaurants to pick up food or beverages.
Q: When are the dances held?
A: Check out our schedule and upcoming dates on the dance page, or visit our events calendar for the latest updates. We host 4-5 community dances each year.
Donations & Support

Q: Can I make a one-time donation instead?

Q: Do donations stay local?
A: Yes. Every sponsorship supports children and families right here in our community.
Q: What if I want to sponsor as a family, group, or business?
A: Wonderful! Shared or corporate sponsorships are welcome. Contact us and we'll make it easy.
Q: How can I support the Cacapon Music and Dance Foundation?

Q: How many students have you served?
A: We provide an average of 20 students each year with partial or full scholarships to study music (300+ hours of music education annually, inspiring 3,650+ hours of practice). Through our Capon Bridge Square Dance program, we serve an average of 400 dancers annually, with daily access to instruments & resources.
Who We Serve
We serve children and youth ages 8–18 from low-income households, with a special focus on families in Hampshire County and nearby rural communities where access to arts education is limited. Scholarships remove financial barriers to private lessons, group classes, and traditional square dance programming so that cost is never a reason to keep a person from participating.
Our primary service area is Hampshire County and neighboring communities in Hardy, Mineral, Morgan, and Frederick County, VA, reflecting the small towns and unincorporated areas where families often travel long distances for enrichment opportunities. Enrollment is open year‑round, and students who enter the program can continue receiving support through age 18 as they grow in skill, confidence, and leadership.
We welcome students who may not otherwise have access to robust music programs—whether they attend public school, are home‑schooled, or learn in other settings—creating chances for ensemble playing, kid jams, and performance that they might not otherwise have. Our programs are designed to be family‑friendly, and parents and caregivers are encouraged to attend, volunteer, and take part in music and dance alongside their children whenever possible.
We are committed to making our programs as accessible as our small, rural venues allow, and we work with families to problem‑solve around transportation, mobility needs, sensory preferences, and other disability-related accommodations in advance of each session or event. Caregivers are always welcome to accompany youth who need extra support, and we regularly adjust class sizes, pacing, and environments to help every child feel safe and included.
Cacapon Music and Dance Foundation welcomes students and families of all races, ethnicities, gender identities, religions, and family structures, and we are committed to providing a respectful, affirming environment for all. Our focus on traditional Appalachian music and dance is rooted in place, but our doors are open to anyone who wants to learn, share, and help carry these community arts forward.
Why Folk Music and Dance Matter
Folk music and dance are more than entertainment — they are how communities remember who they are, tell their stories, and welcome the next generation into something larger than themselves. In our corner of Appalachia, old-time tunes, ballads, and square dances carry the history, humor, and heart of the people who have lived here for generations, and when young people learn this music, they step into a living tradition that connects them to their neighbors, their elders, and the land they call home.
Square dancing matters because it brings people together across age, background, and experience, giving kids, teens, parents, and grandparents a rare chance to be true partners on equal footing. On a dance floor, everyone moves to the same beat, learns the same calls, and shares the same laughter, building confidence, empathy, and a deep sense of belonging with every figure.
In rural communities like ours, distance, cost, and limited programs can keep families from accessing sustained arts education, even though music is proven to support academic success, emotional well-being, creativity, and self-confidence in children. Cacapon Music and Dance Foundation exists to close that gap by offering high-quality, culturally rooted music instruction and multigenerational square dances close to home, removing financial barriers through scholarships and creating welcoming spaces where local traditions are not only preserved, but actively lived and carried forward by the young people who will shape our community’s future.
History of the Foundation
Cacapon Music and Dance Foundation (CMDF) grew out of a lifelong love for the local music of Hampshire County. Executive Director Dakota Karper grew up immersed in the rich heritage of old-time tunes played by neighbors and elders, and she knew early on that preserving and sharing this music was the work she felt most called to do.
After years of offering lessons on the side, in 2017 she began teaching fiddle full-time from her living room. As word spread and more students came, it became clear the community needed a dedicated home for this kind of learning and gathering.
2019 — With the support of friends, students, and neighbors, Dakota built and opened The Cat and The Fiddle, a small roots music school and community hub in Capon Bridge where local and traveling musicians could teach and offer workshops. That same year she partnered with The River House to launch the first "Music Makers" scholarships, helping families who needed financial support to access lessons.
2022 — As demand grew, so did her vision for more folk-centered events, and an individual grant made it possible to start the Capon Bridge Square Dance, bringing multi-generational community dancing back to town on a regular basis.
2023 — The scholarship program and community events had outgrown what one person could hold alone, so—with encouragement from the community—Dakota gathered a small group of dedicated community leaders to form CMDF, which received 501(c)(3) status that same year. Under this new umbrella, the youth scholarship program was rebranded as the Karper Creatives Scholarships and expanded, while the Capon Bridge Square Dance series and other folk events received ongoing support and structure.
Today, The Cat and The Fiddle continues to operate as its own separate studio while partnering closely with CMDF to provide teaching space, instructors, and shared programming aligned with the vision both organizations carry. Together, they have helped dozens of local children access music education, awarded growing numbers of scholarships since 2023, and built a lively calendar of jams, workshops, and dances that keep traditional music at the heart of community life in and around Capon Bridge.
Looking ahead, CMDF hopes to deepen its educational offerings for youth, reach more under-served groups such as aging adults, and continue making accessible community programs—like square dances and jam sessions—a regular part of life for all generations, with a long-term dream of one day having a larger, dedicated home base in Capon Bridge for concerts, workshops, and dances.
